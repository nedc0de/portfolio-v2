How Does a Phone Touchscreen Work? The Simple Answer

By Ned · October 12, 2026 · 3 min read

Your phone’s screen doesn’t feel your finger. It measures it. Every tap is a tiny electrical reading, and your fingertip is part of the circuit.

How a touchscreen works: your finger is part of the circuit

Almost every phone today uses a capacitive touchscreen. The glass carries a thin, see-through layer of a conductor, usually indium tin oxide, with a low voltage running through it, the Institute of Physics explains. When you touch it, your finger draws away a little of that charge. It’s far too small to feel.

That’s also why pressing harder does nothing. The screen senses charge, not force.

A grid of invisible wires

Under the glass, that conductor is cut into rows and columns of electrodes. The phone keeps measuring every spot where a row crosses a column. A finger near a crossing changes that reading, and the touch controller turns the pattern into an x and y position, as a UC Berkeley electrical engineering course note walks through.

Since every crossing gets its own reading, the screen can track several fingers at once. That’s what makes pinch-to-zoom possible.

Why the iPhone ditched the stylus

Older resistive screens worked another way: two flexible layers that touch when you press, which can only register one point at a time, ScienceAlert notes. Lots of them came with a stylus.

Funny enough, Apple is bringing one back. The foldable iPhone Duo will support the USB-C Apple Pencil, just not at launch, MacRumors and TechRepublic report.

Why gloves and water mess with your screen

Regular gloves are insulators, so your finger can’t change the charge through them, according to the Institute of Physics. Touchscreen gloves get around that with conductive thread in the fingertips, ISHN reports.

Water is trickier. It conducts too, so a raindrop can look like a fingertip. Popular Science and Inside Science both explain that phones mix two kinds of measurements to filter moisture out.

How often your phone checks for a touch

The controller doesn’t watch nonstop. It takes samples. At 120Hz, it checks every 8.3 milliseconds; at 60Hz, every 16.6, PhoneArena explained. Apple’s iPhone XS already sampled touch at 120Hz while its screen redrew at 60Hz, Display Daily reported. My iPhone Air’s display refreshes at up to 120Hz, per Apple’s spec sheet.

My take

I tap my iPhone Air all day and never once thought about it doing electrical math under my thumb.

Kind of funny that it can’t tell how hard I’m pressing. Next time it ignores my wet hands, at least I know why.

FAQ

How does a touchscreen know where I touched?

Under the glass is a grid of see-through electrodes in rows and columns. The phone measures every spot where a row crosses a column, and your finger changes the reading at the crossings closest to it. The touch controller turns that into an x and y position.

Why doesn’t my touchscreen work with gloves?

Regular gloves are insulators, so your finger can’t change the charge on the screen through them. Touchscreen gloves work because they have conductive thread sewn into the fingertips.

Why does water make my phone screen act up?

Water conducts electricity too, so a drop on the glass can look like a finger to the sensor. Phones combine two kinds of measurements to filter moisture out, but it doesn’t always work.

Does pressing harder on a touchscreen do anything?

Not for detecting the touch. A capacitive screen senses the charge your finger draws, not how hard you push, so a light tap counts the same as a hard press.
